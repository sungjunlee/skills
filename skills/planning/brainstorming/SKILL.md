# brainstorming

A strong model reaches a reasonable plan on its own, and usually the first one it thinks of. This skill exists to delay that: widen before narrowing, argue with the request as well as the answers, and leave the user choosing among real alternatives rather than approving a default.

## Before anything

Read what the repository and conversation already say — README, docs, code, open issues, notes. Do not ask what they answer. When the idea touches tools, libraries, or products that may already exist, check current sources on the web rather than memory, and cite them.

## Widen

Lay out options that genuinely differ, not variations of one. Among them:

- a reframing — the problem behind the request, stated another way
- the smallest version, or doing nothing, and what that would actually cost
- what already exists and could be adopted instead of built
- a position contrary to where the user is leaning

For a large or contested idea, independent subagents or another model family can widen the field further; say so when you use them.

## Attack

Take each option seriously enough to break it: how it fails, what it costs to own, what it forecloses, whether it can be undone. Say which attacks land and which do not. Keep what you observed apart from what you judge.

## Ask

Ask only when the answer would change which option wins, and ask one thing at a time. When you ask, stop and wait for the answer — do not continue as if it had come. If you cannot ask, list the question as an open decision. If the user hands you the judgment, make the call and say why. Challenge a decision the user has settled once, openly, then respect their answer unless the repository contradicts it.

## Converge, when asked

Stay open until the user wants a direction. Then write a short brief in plain prose, no fixed template:

- the problem as it is now understood
- the options weighed, and why the others lost
- the chosen direction and its riskiest assumption
- acceptance criteria and how to verify them, when the direction is concrete enough to build
- open decisions, and a one-line suggested next step

Stay read-only. Suggest a next step; do not start it.
